# Workshop doc format

The shape used for "Prompt Workshop 1: What Can I Do with AI?" (AIT, Nov 2026). Change it only when his material needs it.

## Section order
1. **Title, date, author**, then a one-line lead: what they will do, how long, what they leave with.
2. **Before you start**: a small table (For / Bring / AI tool / Files / Rule). The files row links the folder; the rule row says not to upload real confidential data to a personal account.
3. **Where this fits**: the series as a numbered list plus a diagram, then "By the end of today you will:" bullets.
4. **Tools and words**: which tools, the ways to use them (with his screenshots), which plan, and a short table of words they will hear. A student's question goes in a quote block with the answer.
5. **The prompt pattern**: a weak prompt, the parts table, the strong prompt, and a diagram that splits the strong prompt into its parts.
6. **Labs**, one heading each (format below).
7. **What we learned**: the habits as a table (habit / say something like / why it helps).
8. **Homework**: a numbered list of what to submit and where.
9. **Resources**: linked titles with the publisher in brackets.

## Lab format
```
## Lab N · <title that says the job>

**Goal:** … **Time:** N minutes. **Upload:** `file name`

<diagram of the lab's flow or inputs>

**Step 1 · <verb>**
> *prompt, in italic, ready to copy*
> *- one bullet per line*

**Step 2 · <verb>** …   (Lab 6 uses "Round")

**Check the AI against the file.** <one line> + a chart drawn from the lab file
**What to look for:** one or two sentences on what the follow-up steps reveal.
**Going further:** (optional) what a connector or the next workshop adds.
**Your notes:**
```
Step 1 is the four-part prompt; steps 2 and 3 push the first answer (challenge it, ask for alternatives, red-team it, turn it into a briefing).
